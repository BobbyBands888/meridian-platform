Every FSBO seller has the same moment of doubt: a stranger texts about the listing, and you realize you're about to let them into your house with no agent, no lockbox, and no one else there. Agents deal with this by doing it hundreds of times. You can deal with it by setting rules before the first message arrives and never bending them.

Here's how Nashville sellers run showings safely, from the first inquiry to the last walkthrough.

## Set your showing rules before you list

Write these down. Put a short version in your listing description. Rules stated up front don't offend real buyers; they filter out everyone else.

- **Appointments only.** No drop-ins, no "I'm outside, can I come in?" A serious buyer will schedule.
- **Two hours' notice minimum.** Enough time to tidy, get a second person there, and confirm the appointment.
- **Showings in daylight or well-lit evening hours.** 9 AM to 7 PM is typical.
- **Name and phone number required to book.** Get it in writing, by text or email.
- **Pre-approval letter before a second showing.** More on this below.

## Screen before anyone gets an address

Your listing should show the neighborhood, not the street, until someone has given you a name and number. On Nashville Buys you can hide the exact address by default; buyers see "Inglewood, 37216" and contact you through the site's form, which logs their name, email, and phone. Only then do you send the address.

When someone asks to see the home:

1. **Get their full name and phone number.** Text back and confirm it's a working number.
2. **Ask one qualifying question.** "Are you working with a lender yet?" or "What's your timeline?" Real buyers answer. People who dodge are usually browsing, and occasionally worse.
3. **Google the name.** Thirty seconds. A real person usually has a footprint.
4. **Confirm the appointment the morning of.** No-shows and last-minute reschedules are common; a confirmation text also reminds them you're organized.

## Never show alone

This is the rule agents follow and sellers skip. Have a second adult in the house for every showing: a spouse, a neighbor, a friend, an adult child. They don't need to do anything but be there. If you can't get someone, reschedule.

If you must be alone, tell someone the appointment time and the buyer's name, and have them check in by text when it's over.

## Prepare the house for strangers

Before the first showing, do a sweep and remove or lock up:

- **Prescription medications.** Bathroom cabinets get opened. This is the most common theft at showings anywhere.
- **Jewelry, cash, small electronics.** Anything pocketable.
- **Mail and documents.** Bank statements, anything with your full name and account numbers. Identity theft starts with a glance.
- **Keys.** Spare house keys, car keys, safe keys.
- **Firearms**, locked and out of sight, always.
- **Family photos** are optional to remove but worth considering; they tell strangers who lives here and when the house might be empty.

Turn on every light, open blinds, set the thermostat to comfortable, and put pets somewhere safe (a barking dog is a liability, and buyers with allergies leave early).

## Run the showing like an agent would

- **Meet them at the door with a sign-in sheet.** Name, phone, date. Ask for a photo ID and glance at it. Nobody legitimate objects; agents ask for the same thing.
- **Let them walk, and follow at a distance.** Buyers need to talk to each other without you hovering, but you should always know where everyone is. Stay near the door; let them go upstairs first.
- **Don't give a tour.** Point out one or two things they'd miss (the new HVAC, the extra storage under the stairs), then step back. Sellers who narrate every room make buyers want to leave.
- **Don't answer the money questions.** "Would you take less?" "What's your lowest?" The answer is always: "Send me an offer in writing and I'll look at it." Verbal negotiation in a hallway is how sellers give away thousands without meaning to.
- **Don't discuss why you're selling.** Divorce, job loss, relocation deadline: all of it becomes negotiating leverage. "We're ready for the next thing" is the whole answer.
- **Don't leave them alone in the house.** Even to grab something from the car.

## The pre-approval rule

Any buyer who's serious has a pre-approval letter from a lender, or can get one in a day. Ask for it before a second showing, and definitely before you spend an evening on a private walkthrough. It tells you they can actually close, and it filters out the people who tour homes for entertainment.

If a buyer says they're paying cash, ask for a proof-of-funds letter from their bank. Same idea.

Buyers who don't have a pre-approval yet can [find a Nashville lender here](/vendors/lenders); most do pre-approvals in 24 hours.

## Open houses: worth it for FSBO?

An open house brings more foot traffic and less control. Neighbors, browsers, and occasionally people casing houses show up alongside buyers. If you do one:

- Two people minimum, one at the door with the sign-in sheet, one moving through the house.
- Everything valuable already locked away.
- A time window of two to three hours, not all afternoon.
- Post it on your listing page, the neighborhood Facebook group, and with a sign at the corner the morning of.

Many FSBO sellers skip open houses entirely and do only scheduled showings. In Nashville's current market, with serious buyers already filtering online, that's a reasonable choice.

## The red flags

End the conversation if you see:

- Refusal to give a name or number
- Pressure to see the house immediately, tonight, "I'm only in town today"
- Interest in what's in the house rather than the house itself ("Do you keep the TV?")
- Someone who asks about your schedule, when the house is empty, or whether you live alone
- A cash offer far above asking, sight unseen, with a request to wire a deposit to you directly. That's a scam every time. Deposits go to the closing attorney or title company, never to you. [How closings work in Tennessee](/guides/closing-attorney-title-company-tennessee-fsbo).

## After the showing

Text a short thank-you within a few hours: "Thanks for coming by — happy to answer any questions." It's polite, and it's your best read on interest: buyers who reply with questions are engaged; silence usually means no.

Keep your sign-in sheet. If anything goes missing later, it's your record of who was there.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to let anyone who asks see my home?**
No. You choose who enters your home, and appointments-only with a name and number is a normal, reasonable requirement. Federal and Tennessee Fair Housing laws prohibit refusing or treating anyone differently based on protected characteristics. Apply the same screening rules to every person, every time.

**Should I use a lockbox for FSBO showings?**
Generally not. Lockboxes work for agent-led showings because the agent is present and accountable. For FSBO, you're the one there, so a lockbox mostly creates a way for someone to enter when you're not.

**Can a buyer's agent show my FSBO home?**
Yes, and it's common. Some buyers have agents even when sellers don't. The agent may ask whether you'll pay a buyer's-agent commission. That's a negotiation between you and the buyer, not a requirement; decide your position in advance and get any agreement in writing through your closing attorney.

**What do I say when a buyer asks why I'm selling?**
"We're ready for the next chapter" or "It's time for something different." Anything more specific becomes leverage against you.

**Is it safe to hold showings as a woman selling alone?**
The same rules apply with more weight: never alone, ID at the door, a second adult present, and someone who knows the schedule. Many sellers in this situation ask a neighbor or relative to be the visible "second person" for every showing.
