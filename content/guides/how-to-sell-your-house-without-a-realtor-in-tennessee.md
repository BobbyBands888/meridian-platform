Tennessee doesn't require you to hire a real estate agent to sell your own home. Plenty of Nashville-area owners sell "for sale by owner" (FSBO), especially when they already have a buyer in mind or want to keep more control over the process. Selling on your own means you take on the work an agent would normally handle, so it helps to know the steps before you start.

This guide is general information, not legal, financial, or pricing advice. Every sale is different, and a Tennessee real estate attorney can tell you what applies to yours.

## 1. Get clear on your numbers first

Before you list, find out what you owe. Your mortgage servicer can send a payoff statement. Subtract that payoff, your expected closing costs, and any repairs you plan to make from the price you hope to get. What's left is roughly what you walk away with.

Nashville Buys doesn't give pricing advice. Many sellers look at recent sales of similar homes nearby (sold, not just listed) to understand the market, and some hire a licensed appraiser for an independent opinion.

## 2. Know the house before a buyer does

A buyer's inspector will go through your home carefully. A pre-listing inspection lets you see the same problems first, while you still control the timeline. You can fix the important items, get repair quotes, or decide to disclose and price accordingly.

In Nashville, it's also worth checking whether past renovations were permitted. Unpermitted work can come up during a buyer's due diligence. Metro Nashville Codes keeps permit records for properties in Davidson County; surrounding counties such as Williamson, Rutherford, Sumner, and Wilson have their own offices.

## 3. Complete Tennessee's disclosure

Tennessee law generally requires sellers of residential property to give buyers a Residential Property Condition Disclosure statement before the purchase contract is signed. There are some exemptions and a limited disclaimer option, and a few items, such as known sinkholes, must be disclosed separately. Our guide to [Tennessee's property disclosure form](/guides/tennessee-property-disclosure-form) explains the basics.

## 4. Prepare the home and your listing

Clean, declutter, and handle small repairs. Neutral paint and good lighting make rooms photograph better. Listing photos matter more than almost anything else online, and many FSBO sellers hire a [real estate photographer](/vendors/photographers) for that reason.

Write a description that sticks to facts about the home: bedrooms, updates, lot size, nearby parks or streets. Avoid phrases that describe who should live there, which can conflict with the Fair Housing Act. If you have an HOA or condo association, gather its documents, since buyers will ask for them.

When you're ready, you can [list your home free on Nashville Buys](/sell). Buyers contact you directly through your listing.

## 5. Handle showings and inquiries safely

Reply to inquiries promptly, and ask serious buyers whether they're paying cash or financing. A pre-approval letter from a lender is a common way for buyers to show they can get a loan. For showings, schedule set times, keep valuables and medications out of sight, and consider having another adult with you.

## 6. Review offers and put the deal in writing

In Tennessee, a real estate sale needs a written contract. The contract covers the price, earnest money, financing terms, inspection and appraisal periods, what stays with the house, who pays which closing costs, and the closing date.

This is where a [real estate attorney](/vendors/attorneys) is especially valuable. An attorney can draft or review the contract, explain contingencies, and flag terms that could cause trouble later. Nashville Buys doesn't draft contracts, hold earnest money, or take part in negotiations.

## 7. Get through inspections, appraisal, and financing

After you sign, the buyer usually has an inspection period and may ask for repairs or a credit. If the buyer is financing, their lender will order an appraisal and underwrite the loan. Respond to requests in writing and keep copies of everything. If the condition of the home materially changes before closing, you'll generally need to tell the buyer.

## 8. Close with a title company or attorney

Tennessee closings are usually handled by a title company or a closing attorney. They search the title, hold earnest money in escrow, prepare the settlement statement, collect and pay out funds, and record the deed with the county Register of Deeds. Property taxes, HOA dues, and similar costs are typically prorated between buyer and seller.

Bring a government-issued photo ID to closing, along with any keys, garage openers, and paperwork you agreed to hand over.
