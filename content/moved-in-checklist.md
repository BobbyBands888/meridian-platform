# Your First Year in Your {region} Home

This is general information, not legal, tax, or financial advice. {brand} connects homeowners with local pros; hire the people you trust and confirm anything tax-related with your county.

## Section 1 — The first week

1. Rekey or change the locks. {#locks} You don't know who still has a key. Rekeying every exterior door, plus garage and gate codes, is cheap peace of mind. (Handymen)
2. Test every smoke and carbon monoxide alarm. {#alarms} Press the test button, replace batteries, and replace any alarm past the date printed on it. Add CO alarms near bedrooms if the home has gas appliances or an attached garage. (Electricians)
3. Find the water shut-off, breaker panel, and gas shut-off. {#shutoffs} Label them and show everyone in the house. When a pipe bursts, minutes matter. (Plumbers)
4. Fix the safety items from your inspection report. {#safety-fixes} Start with anything electrical, gas, water, or structural the inspector flagged, using licensed trades. (Handymen, Electricians, Plumbers)
5. Confirm your deed was recorded. {#deed} Look up your property with the county register of deeds a few weeks after closing, and keep your recorded deed with your closing file. (Attorneys)

## Section 2 — The first three months

6. Schedule the first HVAC service and change the filter. {#hvac} A tune-up before the season you'll lean on it most tells you the system's age and condition, and a fresh filter protects it. (HVAC)
7. Check the water heater. {#water-heater} Note its age from the label, look for rust or leaks around the base, and ask a plumber whether it needs flushing or its relief valve tested. (Plumbers)
8. Get a pest and termite inspection, and ask about a termite bond. {#termites} Termites are common in Middle Tennessee. A licensed pest control company can inspect and explain treatment contracts and what their warranty covers. (Pest Control)
9. Test for radon. {#radon} Tennessee's environment department reports that about one in four Tennessee homes tested is above the EPA action level of 4 pCi/L. A simple test tells you whether yours is. (Home Inspectors)
10. Review your homeowners insurance. {#insurance} Check that coverage matches rebuilding cost, look at your deductible, and ask whether flood coverage makes sense for your address. (Home Insurance)

## Section 3 — Before the seasons turn

11. Clean the gutters and have the roof checked. {#roof} Clogged gutters send water where it doesn't belong. A roofer can spot lifted shingles and flashing problems before a storm finds them. (Roofers)
12. Check the crawlspace or basement for moisture. {#crawlspace} Standing water, damp insulation, or a musty smell point to drainage or vapor problems worth fixing early. (Foundation Crawlspace)

## Section 4 — Taxes and records

13. Know when your property tax bill comes and who sends it. {#tax-bill} Your county trustee (and your city, if it bills separately) collects property tax. Check the trustee's website for the due date, and ask your lender whether taxes are paid from an escrow account.
14. See whether you qualify for Tennessee property tax relief or a tax freeze. {#tax-relief} Tennessee doesn't have a general homestead tax exemption. The state's Property Tax Relief program helps some older, disabled, and disabled veteran homeowners, and counties and cities that adopt the Tax Freeze program hold taxes steady for qualifying homeowners 65 and older. Details are on the Tennessee Comptroller's website.
15. Start a home maintenance log. {#maintenance-log} Record what you fixed, who did it, what it cost, and every warranty and manual. It helps with future repairs, insurance claims, and someday, selling.

A home is a system that ages. Budget a little every month for maintenance, and call a licensed pro for anything electrical, gas, or structural.
