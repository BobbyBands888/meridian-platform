# The Middle Tennessee FSBO Seller Checklist

This is general information, not legal, financial, or pricing advice. Every sale is different; hire your own attorney.

## Section 1 — Before you decide anything

1. Know your number. Pull your mortgage payoff statement (call your servicer; free, takes a day). Subtract payoff, roughly 1–2% for closing costs, and any repairs from your expected sale price. That's what you walk away with. If it's not enough, you aren't ready to list.
2. Pull your own comps. Homes within half a mile that sold in the last 90 days, same bed/bath count, within 20% of your square footage. Sold, not listed. Three to five real comps beat any online estimate. (We don't give pricing advice; this is how to find your own.)
3. Order a pre-listing inspection. (Home Inspectors) You'll find what the buyer's inspector will find, while you still control the timeline and the price. Sellers who skip this negotiate from behind.
4. Decide what you'll fix and what you'll disclose. Split the report into three piles: fix now (safety, water, roof, HVAC, electrical), disclose and price for it, ignore (cosmetic). Write it down. This becomes your negotiating position.
5. Check your title. Ask your closing attorney or a title company for a quick title search. Old liens, an unreleased second mortgage, a boundary issue: better to find it now than at the closing table. (Attorneys)

## Section 2 — Preparing the home

6. Deep clean like you're moving out. Windows, baseboards, inside the oven, grout. Buyers can't articulate "clean" but they feel it in the first ten seconds. (Handymen)
7. Declutter by half. Closets half empty, counters bare, every room with a clear path. Rent a storage unit for a month if you have to. Space sells.
8. Neutralize. Paint anything bold, dated, or scuffed. Warm white or light greige. Fresh paint is the highest-return dollar in the process. (Painters)
9. Fix the list from step 4. Dripping faucets, sticking doors, cracked outlet covers, the burned-out bulb. Buyers count small defects and multiply. (Handymen)
10. Curb appeal. Mulch, trim, pressure-wash the walk, clean the front door, new house numbers if dated. The first photo they see is the front of the house.
11. Stage the three rooms that sell: living room, kitchen, primary bedroom. Everything else just needs to be clean and mostly empty. A stager can do a two-hour consult if you're unsure. (Stagers)
12. Professional photography, no exceptions. (Photographers) Phone photos are the single most common reason a FSBO listing gets ignored. Clear day, lights on, blinds open, mid-morning or late afternoon.

## Section 3 — Paperwork before you list

13. Tennessee Residential Property Condition Disclosure. Required by state law. Complete and honest; "unknown" is acceptable, a lie is not. Download the current form from the state.
14. Lead-based paint disclosure if built before 1978. Federal requirement, separate form, plus the EPA pamphlet. Much of Nashville's housing qualifies. Skipping it is the most common FSBO legal error.
15. Gather your document folder. Survey, HOA documents and fees, permits for any work, appliance manuals and warranties, 12 months of utility bills, the inspection report, the disclosure. One shared folder. Buyers who get answers fast make offers.
16. Line up your closing attorney now, not at contract. (Attorneys) In Tennessee, attorneys or title companies handle closings. Pick one, ask what they charge, and ask them for the standard purchase agreement so you have it ready.
17. Write the listing. Price, beds, baths, square feet, year built, lot size, and a description that names what's actually good: specifics, not adjectives. Our form checks for Fair Housing problems automatically.

## Section 4 — Live on the market

18. List here, then everywhere. Nashville Buys, Zillow's free FSBO listing, Facebook Marketplace, your neighborhood group, a yard sign with a phone number. Your listing link works everywhere.
19. Set your showing rules and stick to them. Appointments only. Two hours' notice. Never show alone. Photo ID at the door, a sign-in sheet. Lock up medications, jewelry, documents.
20. Ask for a pre-approval letter before a second showing. Any serious buyer has one. No letter, no second visit. (Lenders)
21. Don't negotiate at the door. "Thanks for coming, send me your offer in writing." Verbal numbers in a hallway are how sellers give away money without noticing.
22. Respond to every inquiry within an hour, even to say "showings are Saturday." Speed is the one edge you have over agent-listed homes.
23. Reassess at day 14. No showings: it's the photos or the price. Showings but no offers: it's the price or a defect they're all seeing. Adjust once, decisively, not in small steps.

## Section 5 — Offer to contract

24. Every offer in writing, on a real form. The Tennessee Association of Realtors purchase agreement is the standard; your attorney can provide it. Never a text, never a handwritten note.
25. Read the whole offer, not just the price. Financing type, earnest money, inspection period, appraisal contingency, closing date, what conveys, any seller-paid costs. A lower price with clean terms often nets more.
26. Counter once, in writing, with a deadline. 24 hours is standard. Open-ended negotiations lose momentum and buyers.
27. Earnest money goes to the attorney or title company. Never to you. If a buyer wants to hand you a check directly, that's a red flag.
28. Once signed, calendar every deadline. Inspection period end, financing contingency, appraisal, closing. Missing one can void the contract or cost you leverage.

## Section 6 — Under contract

29. The buyer's inspection will produce a repair request. You already know what's on it. Decide in advance: fix, credit, or decline. Credits are usually cleaner than repairs.
30. The appraisal. If the buyer is financing, the lender orders one. If it comes in low: buyer covers the gap, you reduce, or you meet in the middle. Have your comps ready to share with the appraiser.
31. Keep the house in contract condition. Don't remove fixtures you agreed convey. Don't let the yard go.
32. Two weeks out: confirm closing date, time, and location with the attorney. Ask what to bring: ID, all keys and openers, any final paperwork.
33. Review the settlement statement the day before, line by line. Payoff, prorated taxes, attorney fee, credits. Errors are easy to fix the day before, hard at the table.

## Section 7 — Closing and after

34. Final walkthrough. The buyer does one 24–48 hours before. Clean, agreed repairs done, everything that conveys still there.
35. At closing: sign, hand over keys, get your wire confirmation. Confirm wiring instructions by phone with the attorney's office, never by email alone. Wire fraud targets closings.
36. After the deed records: cancel homeowner's insurance (not before), transfer utilities effective the day after closing, forward your mail, save the closing statement for taxes.
37. Tax note. There's a federal tax exclusion on gains from a primary residence you've lived in for at least two of the last five years. Ask a tax preparer whether it applies to you.
